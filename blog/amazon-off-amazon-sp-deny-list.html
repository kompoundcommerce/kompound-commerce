Your Sponsored Products Ads Now Run Off Amazon. Here's the Deny List.

Amazon quietly expanded where your Sponsored Products ads can show up, and it switched the change on for everyone. If you haven't looked at where that spend is going, there's a setting you should know about, and most sellers have never had a reason to open it.

The Lesson: Know Where Your Clicks Come From Before You Pay for Them

Sponsored Products has always been the most predictable ad type on Amazon. Someone searches, your ad shows on Amazon, they click, you pay. That predictability is a big part of why most of us build our accounts around it. So when Amazon changes where those ads can appear, and does it by default, I want to know about it before the money has already been spent.

Since August 10, 2026, Sponsored Products ads can run outside Amazon. Creators in the Amazon Influencer Program can now feature your ads inside their reviews and buying guides. Nothing about your setup changes to make that happen: it's on by default, it uses the same bids and budgets you already set, and you pay the standard cost per click.

My view is simple. A new placement isn't automatically good or bad. But a new placement you didn't choose, can't easily see, and are paying for at your normal CPC is something you should review on purpose, not discover by accident months later.

How It Works: Hidden in Plain Sight

The part that worries me most is visibility. In your normal campaign reports, this traffic looks like regular Sponsored Products spend. There's no separate campaign, no new line item, nothing that jumps out. The only place it shows up is the placement report, under "Off Amazon."

That means if you manage your account from campaign-level views, which most teams do day to day, you could be paying for off-Amazon clicks right now and never notice. Your ACoS might move a little, and you'd blame it on seasonality or competition.

Mansour Norouzi, Partner and Director of Advertising at Incrementum Digital, flagged this on August 14, 2026, a few days after the change went live. What stood out to me in his post was that he said he'd never needed the deny list before that month. Coming from someone who directs advertising at an agency, that tells you how new this problem is.

The deny list, step by step

The control Amazon gives you is the deny list. Here's where to find it and what it does:

  • Where it lives: Administration > Account access and settings > Deny list.
  • How you use it: upload a CSV of what you want to block.
  • What you can block: websites (any domain, including your own), mobile apps, and specific creators. To block a creator, you use the tag ID in their promo URL.
  • What it covers: all active Sponsored Products and Sponsored Display campaigns.
  • How fast it works: it takes effect within about 15 minutes.

Because it's account-wide, you don't have to touch individual campaigns. One upload handles everything that's running.

Block your own website first. If your Sponsored Products ads can appear on your own domain, you're paying CPC for traffic you already own. There's no reason to pay Amazon for a click from a visitor who was already on your site.

How I'd Apply It

Start with the placement report, not the deny list

Before blocking anything beyond your own domain, look at what's actually happening. Pull your placement report for the period since August 10 and find the "Off Amazon" rows. That gives you a real picture of how much you've spent there and what it returned, instead of guessing.

Make decisions on results, not on principle

I wouldn't block every creator by reflex. Some off-Amazon placements may send shoppers who were already researching your category. The point is to make the decision yourself. If the off-Amazon rows are converting at a level you're happy with, leave them. If they're spending without returning anything, block the sites, apps or creators responsible.

Watch for creators that don't fit your brand

Since you can block individual creators by their tag ID, you can be selective. If a particular creator's content doesn't match how you want your brand presented, or their traffic isn't converting, you can remove them without shutting off the whole channel.

Make it part of your regular review

A one-time check isn't enough, because new creators and sites can start showing your ads at any time. I'd add the "Off Amazon" placement rows to whatever routine you already use for search term reviews and bid changes, and update the deny list as you go.

What to Do This Week

  • Pull your placement report covering everything since August 10 and filter for "Off Amazon."
  • Upload a deny list CSV that blocks your own website domain.
  • Review the off-Amazon spend and returns, and block any websites, apps or creators that aren't pulling their weight.
  • Add off-Amazon placements to your regular account review so new spend doesn't slip past you.
