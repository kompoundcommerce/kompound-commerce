Raising GMV Max ROAS Targets: How Bloom Doubled Ad Efficiency

The standard advice on TikTok Shop is to lower your ROAS target and buy volume. I think most brands take that advice too far, for too long. Here's why I'd set targets higher, and how to keep efficiency intact while spend grows.

Low Targets Are a Phase, Not a Strategy

If you run GMV Max, you've probably been told to drop your ROAS target. The logic sounds reasonable: a lower target gives the algorithm room to spend, spend buys reach, and reach buys sales. Early on, when you don't have much content for the system to work with, there's some truth to it.

The problem is that the advice rarely changes when your situation does. Brands keep running low targets long after the reason for them has gone, and they end up paying for volume they could have bought far more cheaply. My view is simple: a low ROAS target is a temporary setting for a specific condition. Once that condition is fixed, the target should move up, and usually by more than feels comfortable.

What It Looks Like in Practice

The clearest example I've seen comes from Joel Contartese, VP of Global Growth at Bloom Nutrition. In a May 21, 2026 post, he described how a TikTok rep advised Bloom to lower its ROAS target. Bloom was running at 2.0, against a health industry average of 3.5. The rep's reasoning was that Bloom didn't have enough content volume, which Joel says was partly right.

Then the content volume arrived, and the advice stayed the same. So Joel moved every campaign to a target of 4.5 or higher overnight, against the advice of both the rep and the agency. Efficiency more than doubled overnight.

He was careful about the takeaway, and I agree with him. He isn't saying ignore your reps. They see hundreds of accounts and often know things you don't. The point is to push back when something doesn't feel right, because the data will tell you quickly whether you were wrong.

The longer view

One overnight result can be a fluke. What convinced me is the year of account-wide GMV Max data Joel shared on October 21, 2026:

  • November 2025: ROAS around 2.5, with TikTok encouraging low targets to drive volume, an approach he now advises against.
  • By January: revenue was flat on roughly half the spend, and ROAS was around 5.
  • By September: ROAS was 4.3, but spend was up about 2.6x, and revenue and orders had more than doubled.

Read that sequence carefully. The first move cut waste: same revenue, half the money. The second move was the hard one: scaling spend substantially while giving up only a little efficiency. Bloom now runs campaigns in buckets with ROAS goals from 3.5 to 7.0 depending on the objective, and Joel says the brand operates at roughly 2x the industry average. In a September 28 post, he admitted that old paid-social instincts had been capping his targets before that.

The real test isn't your best ROAS. It's the ROAS you can hold at scale. High returns are easy at small spend. A slightly lower ROAS at several times the volume is worth far more than a great number on a tiny budget.

How I'd Apply It

Here's how I'd approach ROAS targets on any TikTok Shop account.

Check whether the reason for a low target still exists

If you lowered your target because content volume was thin, look at your content volume now. If it has grown and your target hasn't, you're likely overpaying. The condition that justified the setting is gone, so the setting should go too.

Know your category benchmark

Bloom was running at 2.0 in a category averaging 3.5. That gap was the signal. Find out what's normal for your category, and if you're well below it, treat that as a question to answer, not a cost of doing business.

Bucket campaigns by objective

One target across every campaign forces a compromise. I'd split campaigns by what they're for, with a higher target where you're harvesting efficient sales and a lower one where you're deliberately buying reach. Bloom's range of 3.5 to 7.0 shows how wide that spread can reasonably be.

Judge efficiency together with volume

Don't celebrate a high ROAS on a campaign that barely spends. The highest returns almost always show up at small spend. The skill is pushing volume without efficiency collapsing, so I'd always look at ROAS next to spend, revenue and orders, never on its own.

Treat platform advice as input, not instruction

Reps and agencies are useful. But the platform benefits when you spend more, and your agency may be measured on volume. Listen to them, then test. If you raise a target and results fall apart, you'll know fast and can move it back.

What to Do This Week

  • Write down the current ROAS target on every GMV Max campaign and the reason it was set there.
  • Compare your content volume today with when those targets were set. If it has grown, raise the targets.
  • Look up the typical ROAS for your category and see where you sit against it.
  • Group campaigns into buckets by objective and give each bucket its own target.
  • Build a weekly view that shows ROAS, spend, revenue and orders side by side.
