TikTok Shop in Europe Looks Like the US Two Years Ago

Most European brands still treat TikTok Shop as just another marketplace, and that's the opening. Here's why I think Europe is the best early-stage TikTok Shop market right now, and why the brands that win 2027 are building their audiences there today.

The Lesson: Early Markets Reward the Brands That Move First

Every TikTok Shop market goes through the same phase. The platform is ready, the creators are curious, and most brands are still deciding whether to take it seriously. That window is where the cheapest growth lives, and it closes faster than anyone expects. In the US, that window is behind us. In Europe, it's open right now.

What strikes me about Europe is that the gap isn't the platform. It's how brands are approaching it. Most European brands treat TikTok Shop as one more marketplace to list on, not as a marketing channel that runs on creators and content. That mindset is exactly what slows them down, and it's what creates the opening for brands willing to play it differently.

The takeaway: TikTok Shop in Europe is still in its early phase. Move in quickly, localize properly, and start building audiences in new countries before Shop even launches there.

How It Works: Conservative Brands, Hungry Creators

The clearest picture I've seen of the European market comes from Joel Contartese, VP of Global Growth at Bloom Nutrition. On Jun 6, 2026, after two days with the TikTok Shop EU team in Germany, he described it as feeling like the early US. His read on why is worth spelling out.

European brands are holding themselves back

He found European brands to be conservative in a way that works against the channel. Sampling runs in the tens of units, where it should be hundreds or thousands. Creator briefs are lengthy. Approvals are involved. Many brands give up before the engine has a chance to start. That last point is the one I'd underline: TikTok Shop is a flywheel, and a flywheel that never gets enough push never turns.

Creators are ready, but still developing

On the other side, he noted that creators are excited to work with US brands. Content quality isn't at US level yet, and the creator economy there is still in its infancy. That means the infrastructure to educate creators matters a lot. The brands that teach creators how to make content that converts will get a head start that's hard to copy.

Cultural leverage only gets you the first order

US brands carry cultural leverage that can win a first purchase in Europe. But he was clear that without localization of flavors, pricing and positioning, retention and the halo effect on other channels suffer. Novelty gets someone to try you once. Fit is what brings them back.

What the Early Movers Get

His takeaways read like a checklist for any brand looking at Europe:

  • Move in quick and aggressively. Being early is the advantage. Waiting gives it away.
  • Work with local creators and bring them into your US community. That connects them to what already works for you, instead of starting their education from scratch.
  • Use creator challenges for early volume. He saw them working the way they did in the US 12 months earlier.
  • Look at Super Brand Day. The barrier is much lower than in the US.
  • Lean on FBT. Fulfilled by TikTok is heavily subsidized, with warehouses in Spain, France, Germany and Italy.

Later, on Aug 25, 2026, he shared how Bloom is acting on this. They're building TikTok presence in France, Germany, the UK, Australia, Mexico and Italy. Some of those markets have Shop. In others, they're building organic audiences first, with local, segmented pages. The reasoning: TikTok Shop expands to more countries in 2027, and brands that already have audiences there won't start from zero. As he put it, "Playing for a big 2027 starts now, not in January."

How I'd Apply It

If you're a US brand already working on TikTok Shop, I'd treat Europe as a second chance at the early US. The mistakes are predictable, so avoid them on purpose.

  • Don't import European caution. If you go in, sample like you mean it and keep briefs short and approvals fast. A slow, careful launch on a creator platform usually looks like a failed launch.
  • Budget for creator education. Since content quality is still catching up, plan to teach. Share your best-performing videos, explain the hooks and give creators a direct line to your US community.
  • Localize before you scale. Use the first-purchase pull of being a US brand, but test flavors, price points and positioning for each country early. Retention is where Europe will make or break the economics.
  • Separate Shop markets from audience markets. Where Shop is live, go after sales. Where it isn't, build a local, segmented page now so you aren't starting cold when it arrives.

The biggest shift is in timing. Most brands plan a new market when it opens. The better move is to plan it well ahead and arrive with an audience already waiting.

What to Do This Week

  • List the European countries where Shop is live today and the ones where it isn't, and decide which group each target market belongs in.
  • Review your sampling plan for any European launch. If it's in the tens, rethink it.
  • Check what FBT would cost you through the warehouses in Spain, France, Germany and Italy.
  • Pick one product and write down what would need to change in flavor, price or positioning for one European market.
  • Ask whether Super Brand Day is within reach for your brand.
