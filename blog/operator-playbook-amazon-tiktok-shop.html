The Compounding Playbook: What Two Top Operators Teach About Amazon and TikTok Shop

The sellers winning on Amazon and TikTok Shop right now aren't finding hacks. They're stacking small, correct decisions until those decisions compound. Here's what two operators who share their numbers openly have learned this year.

Why These Two Operators

Most ecommerce advice is written by people describing the work from the outside. The two operators in this series do the work every day and share the numbers in unusual detail on LinkedIn.

Mansour Norouzi is Partner and Director of Advertising at Incrementum Digital, which manages more than $900M a year in Amazon revenue. He also runs his own supplement brand. Joel Contartese is VP of Global Growth at Bloom Nutrition, which became the #1 energy drink brand on TikTok Shop.

We read about 100 of their posts from the past six months. One works mainly on Amazon, the other mainly on TikTok Shop. Their advice still points the same way. Starting tomorrow, we'll publish one article a day breaking down a single lesson from each of them. This post is the overview.

On Amazon: Diagnose Before You Spend

Mansour's posts keep returning to one habit. Before reacting to a number, find out what caused it.

A rank drop isn't always a listing problem

One of his products started slipping in rank with no change to bids, listing or competitors. Putting Search Query Performance data next to ad spend per keyword showed the cause: spend on the top keyword had quietly fallen even though the bid hadn't changed. Click share dropped, then purchase share, then rank. Rank trackers tell you that you dropped. SQP plus ad data tells you why.

Not every keyword is worth losing money on

He sorts keywords into two groups. On rankable keywords, where your product is what the shopper wants, short-term losses buy organic rank that pays back. On non-rankable keywords, which are too broad or too expensive to own, there's no payback, so he never runs them at a loss.

Your LTV is smaller than AMC says

More brands now pull lifetime value from Amazon Marketing Cloud and set acquisition targets from it. Mansour points out that the number shrinks before it should drive a bid: revenue isn't profit, repeat buyers often come back through your ads, the extra customers you win are rarely average, and ad bills arrive long before LTV pays out.

The Prime Day lesson: After Fall Prime Day 2026, revenue across the brands Incrementum manages was up 14% year over year. But everyday sales were up 15.5%, and the event spike was unchanged at about 1.78x a normal day. The growth came from a bigger normal day, not a bigger event.

On TikTok Shop: Build the Engine, Not the Campaign

Joel's posts make one big argument. TikTok Shop isn't a store. It's a marketing engine, and creators are what power it.

Measure it by what moves everywhere else

Bloom doesn't judge TikTok Shop on its own revenue minus costs. It watches Amazon branded search, retail sell-through, organic revenue and DTC traffic, and sees all of them rise when TikTok content is flowing.

Keeping creators is harder than finding them

Bloom's early problem wasn't recruiting creators. It was keeping them. The answer was community: two creator calls a week, one-page briefs that leave room for creativity, in-person events, and rewards for effort as well as sales.

Content volume is the lever, and paid ads amplify it

Joel ties results to daily video count. Around 200 videos a day, winning hooks started to show. Past 1,000 a day, six-figure sales days became realistic. When he paused GMV Max for 30 days in the UK, sales fell 58% but video output fell only 19%. Content is the foundation; ads scale it.

Where They Agree

  1. Compounding beats one-off moves. Joel says Black Friday on TikTok Shop is won in summer. Mansour's Prime Day data says the same thing on Amazon: the brands that grew had a bigger everyday baseline.
  2. AI needs your judgment and your context. Both use AI daily. Mansour caught AI confidently misreading Prime Day data because it didn't know how Amazon attributes sales. Joel removed AI-generated videos from Bloom's creator program to protect real creators.
  3. Own the lever that decides the outcome. Mansour is skeptical of hands-off campaign types that hide their structure. Joel lets GMV Max run itself, but only because Bloom controls what feeds it: a steady supply of strong creator content.

What to Do This Week

  • Check actual spend and click share on your 5 most important Amazon keywords, not just the bids.
  • Label every keyword you push for rank as rankable or non-rankable, and stop running the second group at a loss.
  • Before reacting to a big sales day, check whether it came from new traffic or from shoppers converting carts they filled earlier.
  • If you sell on TikTok Shop, track daily video count next to sales, and put a recurring touchpoint with your creators on the calendar.
