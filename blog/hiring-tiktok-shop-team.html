Hiring a TikTok Shop Team When Experienced Operators Barely Exist

Every brand building a TikTok Shop channel wants to hire someone who has already done it. There aren't enough of those people to go around. Here's how I'd hire instead, and the traits I'd screen for.

The Lesson: Stop Hiring for Platform Experience

Social commerce grew faster than the talent pool behind it. Demand for experienced operators has outpaced supply, so the job posts all ask for the same thing: someone who has run a TikTok Shop before. Most brands are competing for a handful of candidates, overpaying for them, or leaving the seat empty for months while the channel stalls.

I think that's the wrong search. The platform is new enough that time spent on it is a thin signal anyway. What actually predicts whether someone will be good at this job is how they work, not which dashboard they've logged into. The mechanics of the platform can be taught. Ownership, urgency and the ability to work well with creators mostly can't.

That also changes what a leader's job is. If you can't buy experience, you have to build it. Developing people stops being a nice-to-have and becomes part of how the channel grows.

The rule I'd hire by: hire for mindset and teach the platform. Responsibility, leadership, urgency and excitement are the scarce traits. TikTok Shop skills are the ones you can train.

How It Works: A Real Hiring Philosophy

The clearest version of this I've seen comes from Joel Contartese, VP of Global Growth at Bloom Nutrition, who has written publicly about how Bloom built its TikTok Shop team. Their hiring philosophy came down to five traits.

No TikTok Shop experience required

Zero TikTok Shop experience was fine, as long as the person was strong in their role on other channels and willing to learn something new. A great marketplace manager or a strong partnerships lead already has most of the job. The platform-specific part is the easiest piece to fill in.

Good with people

Most roles on the team interact with creators, so being good with people wasn't optional. This is the part of the channel that looks least like traditional ecommerce. Someone who is brilliant in a spreadsheet but can't build a relationship with a creator will hit a ceiling fast.

Proactive

They looked for people who follow up, ask questions and push things forward without being chased. On a platform that changes this quickly, the person who waits for instructions is always a step behind.

Hire for gaps

Rather than cloning the strengths already on the team, they hired to fill what was missing. Joel put it bluntly: "If you're the best at everything on your team, you have the wrong team."

Able to run autonomously

The last trait was the ability to operate independently and own decisions. A small team moving fast can't route every call through one leader.

The same thinking shows up in how Bloom recruited for senior roles. When they hired a Director of TikTok Shop in Austin, in office, the posting looked for creator obsession, speed and autonomy rather than years on the platform. That's the philosophy applied at the top of the team, not just to junior hires.

How I'd Apply It

If I were building a TikTok Shop team today, I'd rewrite the job description before posting anything. Here's how I'd approach it.

  • Look at adjacent channels first. Strong Amazon, DTC, influencer or partnerships people are the most realistic pipeline. I'd judge them on how good they are at their current job and how curious they are about a new one.
  • Test for people skills directly. If the role touches creators, put a creator scenario in the interview: a missed deadline, a sample that didn't arrive, a video that underdelivered. Watch how they handle the relationship, not just the fix.
  • Screen for follow-through. Proactive people leave a trail. Ask for examples of things they pushed forward without being asked, and notice whether they follow up after the interview.
  • Map your gaps before you write the role. List what you and your current team are weak at. Hire for that list, not for a copy of yourself.
  • Give real ownership. Hiring autonomous people only works if you let them own decisions. If every call still runs through you, you'll lose the people you most wanted.
  • Budget time for training. If you hire for mindset, you're committing to teach the platform. Plan for that from day one instead of treating it as a surprise.

My view is that the brands with the best TikTok Shop teams a year from now won't be the ones that found the rare experienced operator. They'll be the ones that hired strong, people-first, proactive operators from other channels and developed them fast.

What to Do This Week

  • Rewrite your open TikTok Shop job description to remove platform-experience requirements and add the traits you actually need.
  • List the strongest people you know on Amazon, DTC or partnerships teams who might want to learn a new channel.
  • Write down the gaps on your current team and decide which one the next hire should fill.
  • Add a creator-relationship scenario to your interview process.
  • Sketch a simple onboarding plan that teaches the platform to someone who has never used it.
