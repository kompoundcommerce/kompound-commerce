Amazon Full Funnel Campaigns: What's Really Under the Hood

Amazon's Full Funnel campaigns promise to run your whole advertising funnel from a handful of inputs. The console shows you something tidy. What sits underneath is a lot messier, and it's worth knowing exactly what you're handing over before you turn one on.

The Lesson: Simple on the Surface Isn't the Same as Simple Underneath

Every ad platform is moving toward campaigns where you set a budget and a goal and let the AI handle the rest. Amazon's version is Full Funnel. I don't think the question is whether these campaigns can produce sales. Of course they can. The question is what structure they build with your money, and how much control you give up in exchange for the convenience.

My view: a campaign type that hides its structure deserves more scrutiny, not less. If you can't see how your budget is being split, you can't judge whether it's being split well. And on Amazon, structure is where most of the performance comes from.

How It Works: What Amazon Asks For and What It Builds

The setup is short. You choose a brand, select at least 3 products, set a daily budget of at least $200, add creative assets (Amazon will suggest some from your asset library or generate them with AI), and pick a start date. From there, Amazon takes over ad formats, how the budget is spread across the funnel, targeting and retargeting, bids, and creative rotation.

Amazon judges success mainly on long-term sales, which combines immediate sales with the projected future value of new customers, plus new-to-brand rate. ROAS is there, but only as a supporting metric. That tells you a lot about what the campaign is built to optimize, and it isn't short-term efficiency.

A look behind the console

The best teardown I've seen comes from Mansour Norouzi, Partner and Director of Advertising at Incrementum Digital. On August 24, 2026 he described Full Funnel as "everything we tell brands not to do," and then his team launched one anyway to see what it actually builds.

In the console, it looked clean: one campaign, one line per product. But when they pulled it through the API, it was a different picture. Underneath sat Sponsored Products manual, Sponsored Products auto, Sponsored Brands headline, Sponsored Brands video, Sponsored Display audience, Sponsored TV and DSP. There was one campaign per ad type, with ad groups split by product. The Sponsored Products manual campaign alone had 12 ad groups.

Inside those ad groups, Mansour found the patterns most experienced managers spend their time avoiding:

  • Every match type mixed together in the same ad group.
  • Long keyword lists stuffed into a single ad group.
  • Branded and unbranded keywords side by side, sharing budget and bids.

What you give up

Put that structure together and the trade becomes clear. With Full Funnel, you give up control over match types, the ability to separate branded from unbranded traffic, control over placements, and the budget decisions themselves. Those are the levers I'd consider most important in any Amazon account.

Mansour's own estimate is that a good manager could get 2-3x more from the same spend using proper structure and control. He's clear that it's a guess, not a test result. His conclusion was fair: can Full Funnel bring sales? Sure. Will it maximize them? He doubts it.

The takeaway: Full Funnel can drive sales, but it gives away match-type control, branded separation, placement control and budget decisions. Never judge it by the console view alone; look at the structure it builds.

How I'd Apply It

Know what you're optimizing for

Because Amazon grades Full Funnel on long-term sales and new-to-brand rate, it's built for customer acquisition, not efficiency. If your priority this quarter is profit, that's a mismatch from day one. If you're deliberately investing in new customers and you accept that ROAS is secondary, it's at least aiming at the same target you are.

Protect your branded traffic

The structure I'd worry about most is branded and unbranded keywords sitting together. Branded searches usually convert well and cheaply, so when they share an ad group with unbranded terms, the blended numbers look better than the unbranded spend deserves. You lose the ability to see what's really driving new demand. I always want branded separated so I can judge each on its own terms.

Look under the hood before you scale

If you test Full Funnel, don't stop at the console summary. Pull the campaigns through the API or your reporting tools and see what was built: which ad types, how many ad groups, how keywords and match types are grouped. You can't evaluate a structure you haven't looked at.

Keep it in a box

If you want to try it, I'd treat Full Funnel as a contained test with its own budget, not a replacement for a structured account. That way your core campaigns keep their match-type, branded and placement control while you learn what the automated version actually delivers for your brand.

What to Do This Week

  • If you're running Full Funnel, pull its campaigns through the API or a reporting tool and map the ad types and ad groups it created.
  • Check whether branded and unbranded keywords are sharing ad groups, in Full Funnel or anywhere else in your account.
  • Write down your current goal for Amazon ads: profit, growth or new customers. Compare it with how Full Funnel measures success.
  • If you test it, give it a separate budget and keep your structured campaigns running alongside it.
