Personalize Your Amazon Brand Store by Customer Lifecycle

Most Brand Stores greet every visitor with the same page, whether that person has never heard of you or has bought from you before. Amazon now lets you fix that without building a second store. My advice: set it up by customer lifecycle first, because that is the version that works on every shopper who walks in.

The Lesson: Stop Giving Strangers and Loyal Customers the Same Pitch

Think about how a good physical store works. Someone who wanders in for the first time gets pointed to the bestsellers. Someone who has been in twice, picked something up and put it back gets help deciding. A regular gets told what's new and what goes with the thing they already own. Nobody serious would run the same script on all three.

Yet that is exactly what most Brand Stores do. One homepage, one hero banner, one set of featured products, served identically to a first-time browser and a repeat buyer. It means the page is always wrong for somebody. Either it is too introductory for the people who already know you, or it skips the basics for the people who don't.

As of October 6, 2026, Brand Stores have a feature called Personalized Experiences: the same page and the same link, with different content shown to different shoppers. That changes what a Brand Store can be. It stops being a static brochure and starts acting like a storefront that recognizes who just walked through the door.

Your Brand Store gets every kind of shopper through one link. Personalizing it by lifecycle stage means each of them finally sees the page that fits where they are with your brand, and you don't have to change a single link or ad to do it.

How It Works: Three Groups, Sorted for You

Mansour Norouzi, Partner and Director of Advertising at Incrementum Digital, flagged this feature soon after it launched and pointed out that few brands are using it. He also put the logic behind it well: a stranger and a loyal customer getting the same pitch is something "a good shopkeeper never would" do.

The mechanics are simple. Amazon automatically sorts shoppers into three lifecycle groups:

  • New to brand: shoppers who have never viewed, carted or bought from you.
  • Considering: shoppers who have looked at your products, maybe added one to their cart, but haven't bought.
  • Purchaser: shoppers who have bought from you.

You don't maintain these lists. Amazon moves shoppers from one group to the next as they act, so someone who was new last week and buys today will see your purchaser version on their next visit.

You can also personalize by channel tag or traffic source. Those options have their place, but they only work on traffic you send yourself. Lifecycle groups cover everyone who lands on your store, whether they came from your ads, a search, a product page or somewhere else. That is why I'd start there.

To find it, go to Stores builder > Explore AI tools > Personalize by cohort.

How I'd Apply It

The useful question for each group is: what does this shopper need from me right now? Answer that honestly and the page content follows.

New to brand: earn the first look

A new shopper doesn't know your range and has no reason to trust you yet. Lead with your best sellers. They are your strongest proof, and they give a newcomer the easiest place to start. Pair that with an introductory deal so the first purchase feels low-risk. Don't bury this visitor in your full catalog or your brand origin story; help them find one product worth trying.

Considering: help them decide

This shopper is already interested. They've looked, maybe carted, and stalled. What usually stops people at this stage is uncertainty, so give them comparisons that make the choice easy: which product fits which need, how the options differ. Then add a limited-time offer to give them a reason to act now instead of later. This is the group where a clear page can move the most shoppers from browsing to buying.

Purchaser: build the relationship, not the discount

Someone who already bought from you doesn't need to be convinced you're legitimate. Show them what pairs with what they bought, and show them what's new. That's how a one-time buyer becomes a repeat customer. What I'd avoid here is heavy discounting. These shoppers already chose you at your price, and training them to wait for a markdown is a habit you'll pay for later.

Keep each version focused. The point of personalization is that every page can do one job well instead of trying to do all three jobs at once.

What to Do This Week

  • Open Stores builder and go to Explore AI tools > Personalize by cohort to see what's available for your store.
  • Write down the one job of each version: introduce (new to brand), decide (considering), deepen (purchaser).
  • For new shoppers, put your best sellers up front and set up an introductory deal.
  • For considering shoppers, build a comparison section and add a limited-time offer.
  • For purchasers, feature complementary products and new arrivals, and pull back on heavy discounts.
  • Leave channel tag and traffic source personalization for later, once the lifecycle versions are live.
