AI-Generated Videos Are Spending Your TikTok Shop Ad Budget

Anyone can make an AI video, tag your product and let GMV Max put your ad budget behind it. That money and attention should be going to your real creators. Here's why I'd treat this as a creator-program problem, and what I'd do about it.

The Lesson: Your Budget Should Back Real Creators

TikTok Shop runs on volume. Once a brand is working at scale, thousands of videos can be tagging its products every day. That volume is the engine, and it's also the weak point: at that scale, you have no real way to control what's authentic and what isn't.

Here's the part most sellers haven't thought through. Anyone can generate an AI video or photo, tag your product and post it. If it gets traction, it flows into GMV Max, and your ad budget starts paying to scale it. You didn't brief it, you didn't approve it, and the person behind it isn't part of your program. But it's spending your money.

I want to be fair about where the line is. Allowing AI content on the platform is TikTok's call. Monetizing that content with your products and your ad spend is a different thing entirely, and that one should be your call. Every dollar GMV Max puts behind a synthetic video is a dollar, and a slice of attention, that didn't go to a real creator who is actually building your brand.

The takeaway: What TikTok allows on the platform and what your ad budget pays to scale are two different decisions. Make the second one yourself, and make it in favor of real creators.

How It Works: One Brand's Response

The clearest example I've seen comes from Joel Contartese, VP of Global Growth at Bloom Nutrition. On Sep 8, 2026, he laid out exactly this problem: AI videos tagging the brand's products and flowing into GMV Max, spending budget to scale themselves. He pointed out that TikTok already detects AI content, and argued that tagging should be gated or brands should get better tools to manage it.

The tooling showed up fast

What happened next is worth knowing. On Sep 9, 2026, Joel got on a call with Samuel Strong and the Cruva team, and by the end of that day they had shipped AI-content detection. It gave brands three options: exclude AI content from GMV Max, set a moderation rule, or review it manually. The point for every other seller is that you don't have to wait for TikTok to solve this. Tools that sit on top of your program can catch it now.

The creators noticed

On Sep 21, 2026, Joel shared that Bloom had started removing AI videos from its program. The reaction from creators was the real story: they messaged almost daily, feeling the brand had their back. His framing was that brands should stop asking how AI can work for them and start asking how it can work for their creators. I think that's exactly the right lens. Protecting creators' share of the budget isn't just cost control. It's a retention move.

Where AI does belong

None of this is anti-AI, and I'm not either. Joel's view, from a Jul 3, 2026 post, is the one I'd follow: use AI behind the scenes for research, operations and workflow, but once it becomes the brand's voice, you lose trust. The day before, after TikTok's AI ad announcements at Cannes, he argued that the brands that win will win with real creators at scale.

How I'd Apply It

Find out how much of your spend is going to AI content

Start with visibility. Go through the videos GMV Max is scaling for your products and look for ones that aren't from creators you know. If you're operating at real volume, don't try to do this by eye forever. Get a tool that flags AI content so you're working from a list, not a hunch.

Pick a rule and write it down

Decide in advance what happens when AI content is found. The three options above are a good menu. Excluding it from GMV Max is the most direct protection for your budget. A moderation rule handles volume without someone reviewing every video. Manual review makes sense if you want a human judgment call on edge cases. Whatever you choose, make it a standing policy rather than something you revisit every time.

Tell your creators what you're doing

This is the step I'd never skip. Your creators can see synthetic videos competing with theirs for the same products and the same paid push. When you clean that up, say so. It tells them their work is the thing you're investing in, and that's the kind of signal that keeps good creators in your program.

Keep AI in the back office

Use AI as hard as you like for research, briefs, reporting and operations. Keep it out of the content your customers see as your brand's voice. That line is simple enough to explain to your whole team.

What to Do This Week

  • Review the videos GMV Max is currently scaling for your top products and flag any that look AI-generated.
  • Check whether your creator management tools offer AI-content detection, and turn it on if they do.
  • Choose your policy: exclude from GMV Max, moderation rule, or manual review.
  • Let your creators know you're protecting their share of the budget.
  • List where your team uses AI today and confirm none of it is speaking as your brand.
