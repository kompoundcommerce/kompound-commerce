Skip Creator Retainers. Invest in the Whole Community Instead.

Paying big retainers to a handful of creators feels like the safe way to win on TikTok Shop. I think it's the opposite. It concentrates your money and your risk in a few people. Here's where I'd put that budget instead.

The Lesson: Retainers Are a Symptom, Not a Strategy

When a TikTok Shop program leans on creator retainers, it usually means the system underneath isn't strong enough to stand on its own. The brand found a few creators who move product, got nervous about losing them, and started paying them to stay.

That feels like protecting your best asset. In practice, it piles money and dependency onto a few people. Your budget gets locked into a small group, your results rise and fall with their posting schedule, and every other creator in your program sees that the real rewards go to someone else.

A strong program isn't a roster of stars. It's a system with a deep affiliate pipeline, where new creators keep coming in, learning what works and growing into top performers. If your business depends on one or two individuals, you don't have a business. You have a risk.

Build a system, not a dependency. Spread your creator investment across the whole community so your results come from a deep pipeline, not from one or two people you can't afford to lose.

How It Works: Spend on the Community, Not the Few

Joel Contartese, VP of Global Growth at Bloom Nutrition, has made this case directly: you don't need creator retainers to win on TikTok Shop, and relying on them signals a weak system. He laid out five places to put that money instead, and I think it's one of the most useful lists a TikTok Shop brand can work from:

  1. A creator coach. Someone who runs weekly calls for the whole community, so every creator gets better, not just the ones already on top.
  2. Tools and subscriptions. Cover the cost of research tools like Daily Virals or FastMoss for your creators, so they can find trends and winning formats on their own.
  3. Events. Dinners in a few cities, and flying your affiliate managers out to meet creators in person.
  4. PR packages, merch and surprise drops. Small, unexpected moments that make creators feel like part of the brand.
  5. Incentives for effort, not just sales. Reward content volume, views, or the best hooks as voted by the community.

Joel's point that ties these together: not everything has to tie to GMV. When you spread investment this way, more people feel included, and included creators keep posting.

He has also flagged a related warning sign. When an agency insists you put high-GMV creators on retainer, treat it as a red flag. In his view, a creator's past GMV with another brand predicts little about what they'll do for yours.

Why This Beats Retainers

I agree with that warning, and I'd add the reason it matters so much. A retainer is a bet on one person's past. Their audience, timing and product fit were different last time. You're paying for a result that may not carry over, and you're paying for it up front.

Community investment works the other way. A creator coach, shared tools and effort-based rewards raise the floor for everyone. Instead of hoping one star repeats a past hit, you're increasing the odds that many creators find their next one. Some of the creators you invest in today will become your top performers, and you'll have built that relationship instead of renting it.

There's also a culture effect that's easy to underestimate. Creators talk to each other. A program where a few people get paid regardless of output tells everyone else where they stand. A program that rewards hooks, volume and effort tells every creator that showing up counts.

How I'd Apply This

Audit your concentration. Look at how much of your creator spend and your TikTok Shop sales sits with your top few creators. If losing one or two of them would hurt badly, that's the risk to fix first.

Redirect, don't just cut. Pulling retainers without replacing them will feel like a downgrade to your community. Move that budget into one or two of the alternatives above so creators see the investment shift, not disappear.

Start with coaching. If I could fund only one item on the list, it would be a weekly community call. It's the cheapest way to lift every creator at once, and it gives you a direct line to what's working.

Reward the behavior you want. Set up a simple incentive for content volume or the best hook of the week, voted by the community. It keeps newer creators motivated before their sales catch up.

Push back on retainer pitches. If an agency or creator pitches a retainer based on GMV they drove for another brand, ask what that actually tells you about your product. Usually, not much.

What to Do This Week

  • List your top creators and calculate what share of your TikTok Shop sales and creator spend they account for.
  • Review any active retainers and decide which you'd rather redirect into community-wide programs.
  • Put a recurring weekly call with your creator community on the calendar.
  • Pick one non-sales incentive, such as content volume or a community-voted best hook, and announce it.
  • Plan one surprise for your creators: a PR package, merch or an early product drop.
