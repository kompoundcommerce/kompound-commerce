Querying Amazon Marketing Cloud in Plain English With Claude

Amazon Marketing Cloud has always held some of the best customer data a brand can get, locked behind SQL most sellers will never write. That barrier is gone. Here's how I'd use an AI model to ask AMC real business questions, and why the question matters more than the query now.

The Lesson: The Bottleneck Moved From Code to Questions

For most brands, AMC has been the tool everyone agrees is valuable and almost nobody uses. The data is there: who bought, when they came back, which promotions brought in new customers and which ones just discounted people who would have bought anyway. Getting it out meant writing SQL against Amazon's schema, or paying someone who could.

That's changing fast. With Amazon's own AMC AI assistant, Claude and ChatGPT, you can now build most of the queries you'd ever need without writing them yourself. Which means the scarce skill isn't technical anymore. It's knowing what to ask.

The takeaway: AI can now write and run your AMC queries. Your job is to bring the right business question, because a precise answer to the wrong question is still worthless.

How It Works: Plain Language In, Answers Out

The best walkthrough I've seen comes from Mansour Norouzi, Partner and Director of Advertising at Incrementum Digital, who shared his setup publicly on Oct 19, 2026. The steps are simple:

  1. Get your own Amazon Ads API access. He thinks every brand should have it, and I agree. If your data only flows through someone else's tool, you're limited to the questions that tool was built to answer.
  2. Connect Claude to the Amazon Ads MCP. That gives the model direct access to AMC.
  3. Ask in plain language. Claude writes the query, runs it in AMC and returns the answer. No SQL on his side.

What the answers looked like

He ran four real questions, two for his own brand and two for a coffee brand. The findings are a good illustration of why this data is worth the effort:

  • A big deal day wasn't what it looked like. Their Best Deal day nearly doubled orders, but 2 of 3 buyers had already bought from them before. On a standard report that's a great day. Through AMC, it's mostly existing customers buying at a discount.
  • Reorder timing was off. Customers reordered later than the team had assumed. That one finding changes when you retarget, when you send follow-ups and how you read early repeat rates.
  • Starting pack size predicted loyalty. Coffee buyers whose first purchase was a 2-pack or bigger repeated about twice as often as the rest. That's a pricing and bundling decision hiding inside the purchase data.

None of those are exotic questions. They're the kind of thing a brand owner wonders about every week and never gets answered because the query is too much work.

Getting the query right for your brand

In June he shared a practical tip for adapting someone else's query: give an AI the query document and tell it, "Ask me whatever you need to customize this query for my brand." Instead of you guessing what to change, the model interviews you about your ASINs, campaigns and date ranges. He also pointed out that AMC's thresholds can hide small rows, and bucketed queries are the way around that.

How I'd Apply It

Start with the decision, not the data. Before you open anything, write down the choice you're trying to make: whether a deal is worth running again, how long to wait before retargeting, which pack size to push in ads. Then ask the question that settles it. The deal-day example is the model I'd copy: the question wasn't how did the deal do? but who actually bought?

I'd focus first on three areas where AMC answers change real decisions:

  • Promotions. Split deal-day buyers into new and returning. If most are returning, you're paying for margin you'd have earned anyway.
  • Repurchase timing. Measure the actual gap between first and second order, and line up your follow-up timing with it instead of with a guess.
  • Entry products. Check whether what people buy first predicts whether they come back. If it does, that's where your acquisition budget should point.

The same setup also reaches beyond AMC. Mansour described building live Claude artifacts for his daily checks: daily sales by product from the Seller API, a price-margin explorer with breakeven ACoS, a competitor price tracker, a promo calendar, a P&L calculator and a launch tracker covering revenue, units, sessions, CVR, ACoS, TACoS and organic vs PPC by day. That's the direction I'd encourage: small, specific tools you actually open every morning, rather than one giant dashboard nobody trusts.

What to Do This Week

  • Check whether your brand has its own Amazon Ads API access, and start the process if it doesn't.
  • Write down the business questions you've wanted answered but never had the data for.
  • Pick one recent deal or promotion and ask how many of its buyers were already customers.
  • Take one existing AMC query and have an AI interview you to customize it for your brand. Use bucketed queries if small rows disappear.
